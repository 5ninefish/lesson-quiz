## Narration

This is Weave. Our example is a lauhala rose.
Lauhala is the dried leaf of the hala tree. Here's a fresh hala leaf, before it's dried.
Hawaiian weavers used lauhala for mats, baskets, and sails, because it can get wet and keep its shape.
A lauhala rose is a small coiled flower, made from a strip of that leaf.
Here's how it's made. Hold one strip as a base. Fold another strip over it, and roll.
Keep folding and rolling. When the coil is the size you want, finish and secure it.
The rose is the result. The steps are what you follow to get there.
This page is virtual, so you don't need real leaves. The photos are your reference, and the model is simplified, not complete weaving directions.
The lesson splits the work into ten photo cards: a symbol on top, and a step underneath.
Here's the cylinder card. Roll the vertical part of the leaf toward the left, along the top edge.
Sequence means the order of the steps. Put the cards in order, and you've made a sequence.
To build yours, tap a card, and it joins your sequence. Tap it again to undo it. Reset clears the whole order.
The full ten-card order is still being checked with a weaver, so your order is saved, not graded as right or wrong.
Now you try. Which order would you try? Pause if you need more time.
Next, a dependency. Choose two cards. Say which must come first, and what changes in the material.
The page gives two clues. The lightning card says, Start. The moon card says, Pau, the finish tuck.
Here's the idea. A step changes the leaf, and that change is what makes a later step possible.
Pick your pair. What has to happen first, and what does it change?
Now, look closely at the cloud card. It says, fold the horizontal leaf backward. But it doesn't say which way.
Two people can follow the same words, and fold differently.
An unclear step can still be part of an algorithm.
Last, a sequence where every rule is on the page: making tea.
Four steps: drink the tea, pour the water, boil the water, and put the tea bag in the cup.
The rules. The water boils before you pour. The tea bag goes in the cup before you pour. And you drink after you pour.
Boil and the bag can happen in either order.
Test an order against the rules. This one pours before the water boils. That breaks the first rule.
Now you try. Build an order that keeps all three rules, then press Check this sequence.
Your photo order is saved, not verified. The tea order is checked against the rules on the page.
