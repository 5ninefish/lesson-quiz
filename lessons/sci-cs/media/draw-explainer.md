# Draw

File: `draw-explainer.mp4`
Captions: `draw-explainer.vtt` (also burned into the video)

Length: 3:38.6 (218.6 s). Target was about 2 to 3.5 minutes, with a hard cap of 4:00.

Lesson slot: Draw (video 4 of 6). It covers Run vs Check, move and turn, building with Add move, Add turn, Wrap in repeat, Undo and Clear, the square task with its prediction, the triangle demonstration (outside turn vs inside corner), the hexagon with the page's "program that does not close", and the rose-inspired pattern task. It has two pause-and-think beats, and the hexagon turn is never stated or drawn.

Every turtle path is computed from its program with the page's rules: start at the dot, heading 0 is screen right, and a positive turn is counterclockwise, which is a left turn on screen. Nothing is drawn by hand or generated. Narration voice: Kokoro (af_heart). Sound effects are synthesized.

## Narration

This is Draw. You'll build three things with blocks: a square, a hexagon, and a pattern.
Run shows the path. Check scores the puzzle named on its button.
First, the two blocks that steer the path.
The path starts at the dot, and the turtle faces right.
Move goes forward, the way the turtle faces. Move 100 draws one straight line.
Turn changes the way it faces. Turn 90 is a quarter turn, to the left. It draws nothing.
Now move 100 again, and the line goes up, because the turtle faces up.
To build that, type a number, then press Add move, or Add turn. Each press adds a block at the end.
For a square, you could add move, turn, four times over. That's eight blocks.
Repeat saves the copying. Clear, then add one move 100, and one turn 90.
Run it, and you get one side.
Type 4 in the repeat box, and press Wrap in repeat. Everything so far goes inside a repeat 4.
Run again. The same square, from three blocks instead of eight.
Undo takes back the last block, or unwraps a wrap. Clear empties the program.
That's the square task. Predict, then build, then Run. The path starts at the dot, and the first move leaves it.
Four quarter turns make 360 degrees, one full turn, so the path closes at the dot. Then press Check the square.
Next, a triangle demonstration, not your next puzzle. Repeat 3: move 100, turn 120.
The three turns still make one full turn. 360 divided by 3 is 120.
The turn is the outside change of direction. The inside corner is a different number. Type the turn, not the inside corner.
Hide this example before the hexagon. It's a different task.
Your practice shape is a hexagon, with six sides. The turns in a closed shape add up to a full turn. Divide by the number of sides.
The page can show a program that does not close: repeat 6, move 100, turn 90.
Six turns of 90 are more than a full turn. The path runs over itself, and ends away from the dot.
Now you try. What turn closes six sides? Build it, Run it, and check that it closes.
Last, a pattern inspired by a rose. It's geometric, not a simulation of weaving.
Vary the repeat, the move, or the turn. Predict what will change, Run, then say what you changed.
Here's one start: move 40, turn 45, in a repeat 8. Eight turns of 45 make one full turn, so it closes.
Change just the turn, to 135. Predict, then Run. Now it's a star. Eight turns of 135 go around three times, and still close.
Or wrap twice: a square, then turn 30, all inside a repeat 12. That makes a ring of squares.
It doesn't have to look like a flower.
Now you try. Which number will you change, and what do you predict? Run it, write what changed, and press Check the pattern.
Each puzzle keeps its own result. The blocks are the program. The drawing is the output.
