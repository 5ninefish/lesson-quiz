In the room

Look at the person who asked. Answer the whole question. If you miss it, ask them to say it again. Speak slowly enough that they can follow. Use an example that matches the question.

Off limits

An employer does not get to ask about your health, or whether you have a disability, unless the job itself states a health requirement. You choose whether to talk about a disability.

About 10 minutes. Three short answers a stranger could follow.

About you

Two or three facts, then two or three things you have done. End with why that fits the role. Use your resume if you have it. If you do not, use a skill you have really practiced.

Strength and weakness

A strength names the skill, not a slogan. “I keep a log when I measure” is specific. “People person” is not. A weakness includes what you are already doing to get better.

Draft all three. You will say one of them out loud on the next screen.

About 12 minutes. Say one answer out loud. Then make it better.

With a partner

They ask, “Why should we accept you?” You answer. They listen for pace, clarity, and an example that fits. They give you a score from 1 to 5. Then you can switch.

No partner: the empty chair

Set a timer for 45 seconds. Say the answer to the empty chair, or into your phone. Play it back if you recorded it. Score yourself. Then say it once more and fix the weak part.

How the score works

  1. 1 Rushed or mumbled. The example does not fit.
  2. 2 Hard to follow. The example is a stretch.
  3. 3 A little unclear. One example fits.
  4. 4 Steady enough. A few examples fit.
  5. 5 Clear, steady, and the examples fit.

Cover three ideas if you can: you can do the work, you fit the team, and you bring something another candidate might not.
